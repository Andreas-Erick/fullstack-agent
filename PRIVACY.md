# Privacy policy and disclaimers

*Last updated: October 26, 2026 · applies to Siren Assistant 2.0 (formerly Nexus Agent) and later*

Siren Assistant is a desktop app for Windows and macOS that runs on your own computer. It has **no server of its own, no account system, no analytics and no telemetry**. The project's authors never receive your data. This page explains what stays on your computer, what goes to the third-party services **you** choose to connect, and the limits of what the software promises.

## What stays on your computer

| Data | Where | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your schedule | `Schedule.md` in the vault | Plain Markdown you can read and edit; never sent anywhere except as part of the agent's context and to devices you paired |
| Settings | App data folder (`%APPDATA%\Siren Assistant`, `~/Library/Application Support/Siren Assistant`, `~/.config/Siren Assistant`; the folder of the app's former name, Nexus Agent, is moved there on the first start of 2.0) | API keys and tokens are encrypted with your operating system's keychain (Electron `safeStorage`) where available |
| Conversations | App data folder → `conversations/` | Encrypted with your system keychain (Windows DPAPI, the macOS Keychain, the Linux secret service), so only your user account on this computer can read them; plain JSON if you turn that off (Settings → Memory & history) or the system offers no keychain. Exports are plain JSON. Screenshots from screen sharing are **never** stored in them |
| Memory, notes, journal, profile, priorities | Your Obsidian vault (default `Documents/Siren Vault`; an existing `Documents/Nexus Vault` stays where it is) | Plain Markdown, versioned with a local git history |
| Skills | App data folder → `skills/` | Plain Markdown |
| Logs and bug reports | App data folder → `logs/` | Known key formats are masked. Bug reports stay local until **you** decide to share one |
| Speech recognition (default) | Runs locally (Whisper via transformers.js) | Your voice is not sent anywhere in the default "local" mode |
| PDF exports | Your workspace folder or `Documents/Siren Exports` (`Nexus Exports` if you already had it) | |
| Paired devices (remote access) | App data folder → `remote-devices.json` | Device name, dates, how and from which IP address it last connected, and a SHA-256 of its token; never the token itself |
| Memory stream | `Stream/` in the vault (one note per month), and `stream-access.json` in the app data folder (when each record was last recalled) | Small observations the assistant notes and the insights it draws from them (by your AI provider, in the background). The most relevant ones go into the agent's context with each message. Turn it off in Settings → Memory & history |
| Inner World (off until you wake it) | `Inner World/` in the vault (Chronicle, each resident's memories, Rehearsals), `innerworld.json` and `innerworld-activity.json` (how many tools the assistant used per village place and day, counts only, the last 14 days) in the app data folder | Fiction written by your AI provider once a day: your personas' names and prompts, how far your real progress has come (counts such as concepts understood or a habit streak), where the assistant mostly worked yesterday (place names and counts, never what the work was) and the residents' memories go into that call. Rehearsals send the scene you describe. Sent to paired devices only while you share your data with them (see Remote access) |
| "Where we left off" note | App data folder → `handoff.json`, and `Where We Left Off.md` in the vault | A short summary of the latest conversation, written when the app closes (by your AI provider, like the journal) and replaced each time. Turn it off in Settings → Obsidian vault |
| Galaxy Watch app | On the watch, and between the watch and your phone | Your spoken question (as text, recognized on the watch by its speech recognition service, Google's, inside the Siren screen; the system's own speech screen only as a fallback), the reply, today's schedule and habits. They travel between the watch and the Siren phone app over Bluetooth through Google's Wear Data Layer (Google Play services on both devices; the messages go device to device, not through a server of ours), and from the phone to your computer like the phone app's own requests. The phone also passes on the active persona's name and theme colors and her face as short recorded animations (made on your computer, kept on the watch until they change or you unpair the phone), and spoken replies as audio from your computer's voice (otherwise the watch's own text-to-speech speaks). Beyond those, the watch keeps nothing after you close the app; the phone remembers only the id of the watch's conversation and which face it last sent. The watch app has no internet permission |
| Custom face on a phone or tablet | Your computer → the paired device | The files of your active custom face, through a link that works for ten minutes and only for the device that asked; the face runs sandboxed (no access to the app, its storage or the network). Nothing is kept on the device beyond the browser's normal page cache |
| Android app | On the phone | The desktop's address and the device token (encrypted with an Android Keystore key, not included in backups). Notifications while the app is closed come over a direct connection to your desktop, not through a push service |

Uninstalling the app does not delete your data folder or vault, so nothing is lost by accident; delete them yourself to remove everything.

## What leaves your computer, and to whom

Only what is needed for a feature **you** turned on is sent, and only to the service that provides it. Each service's own privacy policy and terms apply to what it receives.

| Feature | Sent to | What is sent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI brain | The provider(s) you configure: Anthropic (Claude API or your Claude account via Claude Code), Google (Gemini), DeepSeek, Groq, Perplexity, or a FreeLLMAPI / OpenAI-compatible server you run | Your messages, the conversation context, the system prompt (which includes your persona, relevant memories, vault excerpts, your schedule for the next days, the current date/time and, with the Claude account brain, the names of your Claude connectors and plugins), tool results, and screenshots while you share your screen. In **multi-provider mode** a message can go to the next provider on your list when one is unavailable |
| Screen sharing | The AI provider answering that message | A screenshot of the screen or window **you** picked, only while sharing is on (red "Sharing" badge). Not recorded, not stored |
| Web search and pages | DuckDuckGo (default), or SearXNG, Brave or Tavily if you choose them, and the websites the agent opens | Search queries and page requests |
| Voice output | The offline Kokoro voice (default; runs in the app), or VoiceStudio if you choose it (runs on your computer; when it is unreachable, the offline voice or your operating system's voice) | The text to speak, which stays on this computer |
| Cloud speech recognition (optional) | Groq (Whisper Large v3 Turbo), with the Groq key you enter, or a transcription server you configure | Recordings of what you say, sent when you choose the cloud engine, or (with "use Groq while local Whisper is loading or if it fails") only at those moments. Not your conversations or files. |
| GitHub | GitHub, through the GitHub CLI you signed in to | Reads, and the pushes and pull requests you approve |
| Routines (only the ones you set up) | Whatever their instructions use: the AI brain, and any connector or website they call | Routines run on their own on a schedule while the app is open, with the same tools and approvals as a chat; an approval nobody answers is declined. They are listed (and can be paused or deleted) in the Habits view |
| Skill import (only when you import) | GitHub (api.github.com, raw.githubusercontent.com) | The repository name you give; the skill files are downloaded, nothing is sent |
| Extensions (off by default) | REA: npm (the rea-agents package, downloaded on first use); it analyzes what you point it at on this computer. God's Eye View: your own local copy, which fetches public data feeds (flight, ship, satellite, weather, quake and camera sources, map tiles; Google or Cesium only with your keys) | What their tools are asked: places and areas for the globe, file or app paths for REA. The globe shows in the agent window |
| Internet speed test (only when you click "Measure speed" in the system monitor) | Cloudflare (speed.cloudflare.com) | Test data only: a few downloads and uploads of random bytes, so Cloudflare sees your IP address like any website. The result stays in the app's window and is never sent to the assistant |
| Agent window (on by default; Settings → Internet) | The websites the agent or you open in it | What a page needs from any browser, and what the agent types into it (each click or entry follows your permission mode; it never types passwords or payment details). It has its own sign-ins, separate from your browser and stored in the app's data folder; no camera, microphone, location or downloads, and nothing on this computer or your local network except the globe. What pages show goes to the AI brain like any web page |
| Claude connectors and plugins (off by default) | The services you connected on claude.ai or added as Claude Code plugins (for example Todoist, Google Calendar, Canva, Slack), through Claude Code and your Claude account | What the agent puts into each call you approve (a task, a search, a design request…). Each call asks you first unless you ticked "always allow" for that service in Settings → AI brain. What the services return goes to the AI brain like any tool result |
| Update check (on by default; Settings → Memory & history) | GitHub (api.github.com) | One request a day for the newest release of Siren: GitHub sees your IP address and the app's version (in the request's user agent), like any website. Nothing is downloaded or installed until you click Download, which opens the release in your browser. Paired phones learn about a new Android app from this computer and never ask GitHub themselves |
| Model downloads | Hugging Face | Downloads the speech, voice-detection and offline-voice models (and the offline voices) once; no personal data. The runtime that runs them ships with the app. |
| Remote access (off by default) | Only the devices **you** pair, directly (this computer, or your local network if you allow it); no server of ours in between | Your conversations, the replies as they stream, approval requests, your schedule (read-only, only the days the phone shows), the agent's name and theme colors, and, while "Show my notes, memories… on paired devices" is on (Settings → Remote access; on by default, off in one click): today's overview (schedule, habits, Active Priorities, cards due, where you left off), your habits (which you can check off from the phone), your flashcard decks and their cards with their images and sounds (which you can study from the phone: your answers are saved on this computer), learning topics and decks, the skill tree, your memories and memory stream, your vault notes (read-only, inside the vault only), the Inner World's day, your skills and routines (names and schedules), and the map of the app's own architecture (the structure of its code: file names, function names and their connections, nothing of yours). Trimmed copies: never file paths outside the vault, private habit notes or anything not listed here; and spoken replies (made on this computer by the offline voice or VoiceStudio) if you turn them on. Tool activity shows as names only: what a tool was given or returned (file contents, commands, notes) stays on this computer. Never settings, keys, the setup conversation or screenshots of this desktop. A device unused for 90 days is unpaired automatically. On the local network the connection is not encrypted yet. The phone keeps only its device token, the open conversation's id and the 🔈 choice, in its browser storage. Push-to-talk recordings (secure address only) are transcribed on this computer with your speech-to-text setting and not kept |
| Secure remote address (optional) | Cloudflare (Tunnel, and Access if you set it up) or Tailscale, under **your** account | The same traffic as above, encrypted from the phone to Cloudflare's or Tailscale's network and on to this computer. Cloudflare can see the traffic it relays (it ends HTTPS at its edge); Tailscale connections are end-to-end encrypted. The temporary Cloudflare address is public: protect it with pairing and turn it off after testing |

The app never sells, shares or uploads data on its own initiative. The private-network block in web fetching, the approval gate for consequential actions, and the "external content is data" rule are described in the [README](README.md#safety-and-permissions).

## Accepting this policy

The setup wizard shows a short version of this page and asks you to accept it before you continue; the app only records when you did (`privacy.acceptedAt` in your settings, on this computer).

## Your choices

- Use **local** speech recognition and a **local** model server to keep conversations entirely on your computer.
- Turn features off in **Settings** (internet, calendar, GitHub, Claude connectors, journaling, screen-sharing screenshots per message, the daily update check).
- Remove individual memories, notes, conversations and skills at any time, or clear them all in **Settings → Memory & history**.
- Delete API keys in each provider's console. (Earlier versions could sign in to Google Calendar; that is gone, and its saved sign-in is deleted at launch. You can also remove the app at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).)

- **AI output can be wrong.** Language models make mistakes and can state false things confidently. Check important information (medical, legal, financial, safety-related) with a qualified source. The agent is not a professional adviser.
- **Actions on your behalf.** The agent can add to your schedule, write notes, change files in its workspace, push code to GitHub and change its own code, within the permissions you set. Consequential actions ask for approval in "Ask" mode; in "Auto" mode many do not. You are responsible for what you approve and for the permission mode you choose.
- **Self-modification.** Code changes the agent makes to itself are recorded and can be reverted, but they can still break the app; `npm run recover` undoes the last change. Installing an update replaces the app's files, including changes the agent made to itself.
- **Third-party services and costs.** You are responsible for complying with the terms of every service you connect (including acceptable-use policies and rate limits) and for any charges they bill. Multi-provider mode can send a message to a paid provider when a free one is unavailable; order and switch providers in Settings → AI brain.
- **Other people's data.** If you share your screen, calendar, documents or voice recordings that contain other people's information, make sure you are allowed to. Only clone a voice that is yours or whose owner agreed.
- **Security has limits.** The app is designed defensively (approvals, sandboxed content, encrypted secrets, a token for every paired device, checks on every remote request), but no software is free of vulnerabilities, and the authors cannot guarantee that it is. Keep your operating system, the app and your devices updated, use a screen lock, and report problems as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
- **Remote access and paired devices.** Turning on remote access, the local network or a secure address (Tailscale, Cloudflare) makes the assistant reachable from your paired devices; you are responsible for those devices and for the accounts of the tunnel service you choose. A paired phone, tablet or watch shows your conversations, schedule, habits and notes to whoever can unlock it. If a device is lost, remove it in Settings → Remote access (or "Remove all devices"). A device can also be made view only there, and the computer notifies you when a device is paired or connects from a network it never used. In the Android app, the app lock asks for the phone's own unlock (fingerprint, face or screen lock; the app never sees biometric data).
- **Not for high-risk use.** Do not use Siren Assistant where a failure could cause injury, significant financial loss or harm to others.
